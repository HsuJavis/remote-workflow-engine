# Design panel — Adversarial group (r1, independent)

**Stage:** Detailed Design (Gate 3/4 merged), iteration **v3** (ARCH-015..018 / REQ-016..020, plus the three cross-cutting amendments D-DOS global RunGuard semaphore, D-BIND non-loopback guard, D-PROC subprocess lifecycle). `03-tasks.md` for v3 does not exist yet — where task-splitting affects my lens I say so under **Task-split guidance**.
**Lenses carried (traded off explicitly):** (a) Interface-contract — signatures, I/O types, compatibility; (b) Boundary/error — failure modes, error codes, boundary completeness; (c) Testability — every DES coverable by a UT, clock/storage/spawn injectable.
**Tie-break:** Karpathy simplicity-first (minimum design that solves the REQ; no speculative flexibility).
**Round:** 1 (independent — I argue from my lenses and surface conflicts; the synthesizer reconciles).
**Scope note:** v1/v2 kernel (DES-001..023) is DONE and real-validated. I extend its established contracts — one `ResultEnvelope` `{runId,status,result,error?}`, one injected `Clock`, injectable `RunStore`/`GatewayClient`/`spawnImpl`/`McpProbe` ports — and flag where v3 must not violate them.

## Altitude judgment (system vs agent)
From `tech_stack` + REQ-016..020 this is **both**, and the v3 slice splits cleanly by module — apply the fitting altitude per module, don't force the other:
- **Conventional-system altitude:** MCP Provisioning Registry CRUD + typed errors (ARCH-015), Secret store loader (ARCH-016 source half), the D-BIND loopback guard, the D-PROC port-selection/SIGTERM→SIGKILL/temp-dir lifecycle — ordinary store/config/process engineering; the load-bearing content is injectability and boundary-predicate correctness.
- **AI-agent altitude:** the SDK Session-Options Builder (ARCH-017) is the heart of the slice — it decides *what an agent runtime sees* (thinking mode, curated tool allowlist, injected-MCP set, resolved secrets) for **non-Anthropic models running the full harness**. `SessionInitRecord` is agent-altitude observability (what the model was actually given); `FailureEnvelope` is agent-altitude replaceability/self-sustainability (why an `agent()` resolved `null` instead of fake success). ARCH-018 asset-ingestion policy mutates agent runtimes (skills materialized, hooks refused, MCP redirected).
The axis v3 most stresses at the **agent altitude** is self-sustainability under an *unattended, RCE-capable, no-auth* surface: the SDK harness spawns a CLI subprocess per `agent()` plus N stdio-MCP children per session, the scheduler fires runs autonomously, and secrets flow into those children. I apply both altitudes and force neither (no OIDC ceremony pulled forward against D5; no human-UX polish on `mcp_provision`).

## Summary
The v3 seam choices are mostly right and I endorse the load-bearing ones: the ARCH-017 **pure-builder ⟂ impure-spawn split** as the master v3 test seam (`(providerClass, alias, config, provisionedRefs, resolvedSecrets) → SDKOptions` — everything but the one real Ollama `tool_use` round-trip becomes a pure UT); the **flat config-driven `ProviderProfile`** over a provider SPI (D-PROFILE — it is incurred debt from the D-F6 thinking-400, not speculative); the **global RunGuard semaphore** promotion (D-DOS); **kill-on-timeout, not abandon** (D-KILL); the **typed reason-code taxonomy** with FailureEnvelope-never-fake-success; and **hooks-drop-by-construction** (ARCH-018) as the cleanest way to close the RCE vector.

My independent objections concentrate where the design **over-claims purity, under-specifies a boundary, or lets a lifecycle guarantee straddle a seam**. The five that matter, ranked:
1. **Kill-on-timeout slot accounting is a double-free / never-free hazard** (D-DOS × D-KILL intersection) — the single highest-risk boundary in the slice.
2. **Secret redaction races the transcript tap** — the AgentTranscriptSink taps the raw SDK stream; a secret in an MCP child's argv/env can land in `agent-<id>.jsonl` *before* redaction unless redaction is specified at capture time.
3. **`ProviderProfile`'s default for an unprofiled alias must fail *safe*** (thinking disabled), or a future alias re-introduces the exact D-F6 Ollama 400.
4. **The D-BIND guard predicate must be is-loopback, not `=== '127.0.0.1'`** (::1, 127.0.0.2) — a string compare is both a false-reject and a bypass.
5. **Secret resolution must be atomic (all-or-nothing)** — a partial-resolve that injects one good handle and passes another through fails-open on the very invariant REQ-018 states.

## Key points

### Interface-contract lens
- **Endorse the ARCH-017 builder signature and its purity as a hard contract.** `(providerClass, alias, config, provisionedRefs, resolvedSecrets) → SDKOptions` is right *only if* `resolvedSecrets` is genuinely passed in and the builder reads **no** env/global/clock. Make purity a stated DES invariant with a UT that runs the builder with a frozen input and asserts determinism. If the builder ever reaches into `process.env` "for convenience," the master seam rots and the slice loses its cheap-UT property. This is the one contract I would gate the whole slice on.
- **Keep `GatewayClient.invoke(prompt,opts)` frozen.** SDKOptions must never leak up through ARCH-004 into orchestration (the untrusted child already can't see it; the trusted Agent Executor shouldn't either). ARCH-017 lives *inside* the two GatewayClient impls. This preserves D2 replaceability: swapping the gateway is still a one-interface change.
- **`SecretResolver` should be a pure `resolveConfig(config, secretSource) → resolvedConfig` function, not a stateful resolver that reaches global env.** Signature: walk a config object, replace every `${secret:name}`, throw `SECRET_MISSING`/`SECRET_HANDLE_INVALID` on the first bad handle. The env/`LoadCredential` load into parent memory is a *separate* source object handed in — that keeps the resolver a pure UT and the source loader a thin adapter (testability follows the split).
- **`ProviderProfile` is the single source of truth shared with the ARCH-008 alias validator.** Fields `{supportsExtendedThinking, supportsToolUse, timeoutMs, retries, effortMapping}`. The validator reads the same table it feeds the builder — one row per alias, no second copy. This also retires the open `effort`-mapping question as a config row.
- **Contract gap — `mcp_provision`'s "admin-only" authority is not in its signature.** It is enforced *only* by network position (D-BIND loopback). The tool's contract should state this precondition explicitly and, per my boundary lens below, refuse to serve when `bind != loopback` even if `insecureNoAuth:true` is set for the general listener — an RCE-provisioning write authority deserves a stricter default than a read.

### Boundary/error lens
- **(#1) Slot free-exactly-once on the kill path.** D-DOS rations a *process-global* semaphore slot per agent; D-KILL kills the CLI child on timeout. The failure surface: (a) the `Promise.race` timeout branch frees the slot AND the query promise later settles and frees it again → **double-free**, semaphore over-counts, caps silently exceeded; (b) kill throws / child already exiting and the free is on the query-settle path only → **never-free**, a hung provider starves the resource the semaphore rations (the exact "hung provider starves the semaphore" hazard D-DOS names). **Design requirement:** slot acquire in the RunGuard, release in a single `finally` keyed to the *race outcome*, idempotent (guard flag), covering success / schema-retry-exhausted / provider-error / timeout-kill / stop / suspend. One release site, provably once. Make it a UT with an injected semaphore (max=1) that asserts count returns to 0 on every branch.
- **(#2) Redaction is a capture-time boundary, not a post-filter.** REQ-018 says resolved values never enter transcripts/dashboard/logs. But secrets are injected into the *config the parent hands the SDK* — including possibly a stdio-MCP child's argv/env — and the AgentTranscriptSink taps the raw SDK message/event stream. **Design requirement:** a pure `redact(event, secretValues) → event` in the capture path, applied before any write to `agent-<id>.jsonl`; and `SessionInitRecord` logs secret **handle names only**, never values. Testable invariant: given a config with `${secret:x}`, no byte of the resolved value of `x` appears in the transcript or init record. This is a distinct requirement from "the sandbox can't read the proxy config file" (that's the ARCH-016 filesystem-containment half) — both are needed; neither substitutes for the other.
- **(#3) Fail-safe `ProviderProfile` default.** An alias with no profile row must default to `supportsExtendedThinking:false` and conservative `supportsToolUse`, because the D-F6 defect was *defaulting thinking on*. A missing profile should ideally be caught at submission by ARCH-008 (`ALIAS_PROFILE_MISSING`), but if it ever reaches the builder, the default must not 400 a non-reasoning model. Fail-safe default > fail-open default.
- **(#4) `isLoopback(bind)` predicate, not string equality.** Must accept `127.0.0.0/8` and `::1`, reject everything else, and not be foolable by `127.0.0.2` reading as "loopback-ish" in a naive check. `bind !== '127.0.0.1'` is simultaneously a false-reject (breaks a legitimate `::1` deploy) and, if someone "fixes" it with a prefix match, a bypass. One tested predicate.
- **(#5) Atomic secret resolution.** Resolve the *whole* config or throw; never inject a partial config. A config with one good and one missing handle must fail the session build with `SECRET_MISSING`, not build a session with one secret live and the other handle passed through literally. Boundary UT: mixed-handle config → build throws, nothing spawned.
- **Runtime-dead MCP is a distinct failure mode from `MCP_NOT_PROVISIONED`.** D-PROBE probes at provision and optionally at submission, but a provisioned-and-live MCP can die before the run. That surfaces inside the SDK session as a tool failure → `FailureEnvelope{kind:tool_error}` → `agent()` null. That is acceptable (matches REQ-003 null semantics) but the design must *say so* so it isn't mistaken for a silent no-op. Do **not** mint a new public reason code for it (see conflict C2); keep it in `FailureEnvelope.kind`.
- **Reason-code completeness check** against REQ-016..020: `MCP_NOT_PROVISIONED`, `MCP_PROBE_FAILED`, `SECRET_MISSING`, `SECRET_HANDLE_INVALID`, `HOOKS_UNSUPPORTED`, `PROVIDER_TIMEOUT` cover the stated clauses. Add `ALIAS_PROFILE_MISSING` (submission-time, from #3). Everything finer lives internal in `FailureEnvelope`, not the client envelope.

### Testability lens
- **Timeout must ride the injected Clock, not real ms.** REQ-020's `Promise.race(query, timeoutMs)` must use the same injected Clock/Ticker seam v2 already established (DES-016), or the UT waits the CLI's real ~4-minute backoff. Parameterize the delay; assert the race resolves `null` at simulated `timeoutMs` with the child killed.
- **Kill is an injected `spawnImpl`/`killImpl`, asserted once.** The `queryImpl`/`spawnImpl` seam already exists per tech_stack. The kill target must be an injectable handle so a UT asserts `kill` called exactly once on timeout and the slot freed exactly once — you cannot unit-test killing a real `claude` CLI child, so the design must route kill through the seam.
- **`McpProbe` injected into `mcp_provision`.** Provision-time probe is a network/subprocess action; the v2 `McpProbe` port already isolates it. UT: provision with a fake prober returning dead → `MCP_PROBE_FAILED`, nothing persisted; live → row persisted.
- **The global semaphore must be an injected instance, not a module-level `static`.** This is the one place testability and the "process-global" contract collide (conflict C1): resolve by constructing **one** instance at the composition root and passing it by reference into every RunGuard — same object (true global rationing), still injectable/resettable (a UT builds one with max=2 and asserts the 3rd agent queues). Never a `static`/singleton-module global; those resist isolation and leak state across tests.
- **Secret-never-in-artifact and curated-allowlist-observable are pure UTs** over `SessionInitRecord` + `redact` — REQ-016 c3 ("curated surface observable in session init") becomes auditable from the store with no live model. Only the real `tool_use` round-trip against local Ollama needs the real tier.

## Task-split guidance (feeds the synthesizer's 03-tasks)
Split along the test seams so "every DES coverable by a UT" stays honest — never merge a pure and an impure half into one task:
- **ARCH-017 → three tasks:** (a) pure builder + `ProviderProfile` table + validator wiring (all pure UTs, incl. thinking-disabled, curated allowlist, strict-MCP, fail-safe default); (b) impure timeout-race + kill-on-timeout + slot free-exactly-once over injected clock/spawn/semaphore; (c) the one real-tier Ollama `tool_use` round-trip (VAL). Merging (a) into (b) forfeits the master seam.
- **ARCH-015 → two tasks:** (a) registry CRUD + typed `MCP_NOT_PROVISIONED`/`MCP_PROBE_FAILED` + strict-MCP referenced-by-name injection (store UTs); (b) `McpProbe` wiring at provision (injected-probe UT).
- **ARCH-016 → two tasks:** (a) pure `resolveConfig` + `redact` (pure UTs, atomicity, redaction); (b) env/`LoadCredential` source loader + parent-only filesystem containment callback (the realpath/arg-complete/`Bash`-deny hardening).
- **D-DOS / D-BIND / D-PROC** are amendments to existing modules (RunGuard / ARCH-009 / ARCH-005+017), each a small tightly-scoped task with its own boundary UT (semaphore count-returns-to-zero; `isLoopback` truth table; port-selection + SIGTERM→SIGKILL + temp-dir cleanup).

## Risks
- **R1 (HIGH) — semaphore slot double-free / never-free on the kill path.** Caps silently violated (double-free) or a hung provider starves all runs (never-free). Mitigation: single idempotent release site keyed to race outcome; UT count-returns-to-zero on every branch. This is the risk both groups flagged (D-KILL "REQ-020 passes its test while budget silently rots").
- **R2 (HIGH) — secret leak via the transcript tap** before redaction, or via a stdio-MCP child's argv appearing in the SDK event stream. Mitigation: capture-time `redact`, handle-names-only `SessionInitRecord`, invariant UT.
- **R3 (MED) — unprofiled-alias thinking default re-opens the D-F6 Ollama 400.** Mitigation: fail-safe default + submission-time `ALIAS_PROFILE_MISSING`.
- **R4 (MED) — `isLoopback` string-compare false-reject/bypass.** Mitigation: one tested predicate over `127.0.0.0/8` + `::1`.
- **R5 (MED) — partial (non-atomic) secret resolution fails open** on REQ-018's core invariant. Mitigation: resolve-whole-or-throw.
- **R6 (LOW) — builder purity erosion** (env read inside the "pure" builder) silently degrades the whole slice's UT economics. Mitigation: purity invariant UT; ban env access in the builder in review.
- **R7 (LOW) — `mcp_provision` write authority equated with a read on the no-auth listener.** Mitigation: stricter default (refuse when `bind != loopback` even with `insecureNoAuth`).

## Expected disagreements with the other lens (quality-dimensions: observability / replaceability / consumability / self-sustainability)
- **ProviderProfile: config table vs provider SPI.** Quality/replaceability will likely push toward a formal provider plugin/SPI for future providers. I hold the Karpathy line (D-PROFILE already resolved): a **flat config table**, one row per alias — a real defect's remedy, not a plugin framework. GLM/qwen is a config row.
- **Subprocess lifecycle: in-process supervisor vs supervisor-owned restart.** Quality/self-sustainability may want a `ManagedSubprocess` watchdog. I side with D-PROC: **minimal in-process cleanup only** (port selection, health probe, SIGTERM→SIGKILL, temp-dir + kill-on-timeout slot free — all incurred debt), **restart/recovery stays on systemd/docker restart-policy**. The line: per-agent kill+slot-free is in-process (the server stays up, it's not the supervisor's job); process *restart* is the supervisor's. Expect a boundary argument about exactly where that line sits.
- **Discoverability vs redaction on the no-auth listener.** Quality/consumability will want `list-provisioned-MCPs` / `list-secret-names` endpoints for good agent UX. My boundary/security lens refuses enumeration on the unauthenticated listener (D-REDACT): a **clear typed error to the submitter, but no inventory enumeration** until REQ-012 auth lands. Expect this to be the sharpest disagreement.
- **`SessionInitRecord` richness.** We agree it should exist and be rich (agent-altitude observability). We will disagree on contents: quality may want the resolved effective config for debuggability; I insist **handle-names-only, redacted values** — observability must not become the leak.
- **Global semaphore default value.** A scalability-leaning reviewer may argue the `min(16,cores-2)` default; I care far more that it is **injectable and count-provable** than about its number, and will trade the exact default for the testable-instance contract.

## Internal conflicts within my own group (surfaced per the debate mandate)
- **C1 — Interface-contract ⟂ Testability on the global semaphore.** Contract wants "one process-global counter"; testability wants an injectable, resettable instance. **Karpathy resolution:** one instance built at the composition root, passed by reference — same object, injected seam, no `static` global. Minimal, satisfies both.
- **C2 — Boundary/error ⟂ Interface-contract on reason-code granularity.** Boundary wants a distinct public code per failure mode (runtime-dead MCP, kill-failed, partial-secret); consumability wants a small stable enum agents branch on. **Karpathy resolution:** keep the **small published taxonomy** (7 codes) on the client envelope; put finer distinctions in the internal `FailureEnvelope.kind/providerDetail` (store-only). Don't multiply client-facing codes speculatively.
- **C3 — Boundary/error ⟂ simplicity on kill lifecycle.** Boundary wants full lifecycle guarantees (kill, reap zombie, free slot, attribute partial tokens, cleanup temp dir); D-PROC says "no in-process watchdog." **Resolution:** the minimal per-agent cleanup (kill + finally-free-slot + temp-dir rm) *is* warranted (incurred debt, orphan burns tokens/slot), broader self-heal stays on the supervisor. Draw the line at "one agent's resources" (in-process) vs "the process" (supervisor).
- **C4 — Testability ⟂ Interface-contract on redaction.** To unit-test "no secret in transcript" you need capture to be a pure function of `(event, secretValues)`; the live SDK stream is async. **Resolution:** a pure `redact(event, secretValues)` sits in the tap — unit-testable alone; the live capture just applies it. Testable redaction without mocking the whole SDK.
